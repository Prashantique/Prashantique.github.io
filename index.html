About

I am Prashant Sharma, a certified ethical hacker and bug hunter based in Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh. My work is web application security: finding the flaw, proving it, and writing it up so the person who has to fix it knows exactly what to do.

That last part is why I build tools. Roughly three quarters of an engagement is not testing — it is evidence, impact, remediation and report. Most online security tools tell you what is wrong and stop there. Mine give impact, reproduction steps, remediation and report-ready output, because that is the part that actually costs time.

The toolkit is deliberately constrained. It runs in the browser with no framework, no CDN and no analytics, and every tool has a paste mode that makes no network request at all. That is what makes it safe to hand a link to a client so they can reproduce a finding themselves. Where a browser genuinely cannot do something — enumerating TLS cipher suites, scanning ports — the tool says so rather than pretending.

Principles

  • 01Measure feasibility before promising a capability
  • 02Withhold a verdict rather than guess one
  • 03State plainly what a tool cannot determine
  • 04One tool should absorb a whole workflow
  • 05Finish at report-ready output, never before
